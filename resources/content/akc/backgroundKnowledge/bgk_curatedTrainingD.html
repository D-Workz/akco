Curated and Training data

Curated or training data plays a crucial role in supervised machine learning techniques, including: embedding-based, path-based, verbalization-based, hybrid, and statistical approaches.

This data may originate from the knowledge graph itself or from external sources. It generally consists of a training set (used to learn model parameters) and a validation set (used to evaluate performance). These datasets help models learn specific patterns to detect errors or predict missing entities. Training typically concludes when the model converges—that is, when further improvements on the validation set stop. If the model performs well on training data but poorly on new data, it may be overfitting.

Data curation is a critical preprocessing step that may involve manual evaluation ([Jeyaraj et al., 2019]). Its goals are to ensure quality, reduce bias, include representative negative examples, and ensure accurate labeling.

In addition to training, curated data is used to evaluate the correctness of predictions and model outputs (as noted in [Paulheim, 2017]).
