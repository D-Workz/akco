Cleaning approaches can utilize either internal or external methods, as described by [Paulheim, 2017].

Internal Methods

Internal methods use the knowledge graph itself as background knowledge. This may include leveraging its graph structure through unsupervised machine learning or statistical analysis, or utilizing its ontology via logical reasoning.

These methods depend on the inherent quality of the knowledge graph—especially of specific subgraphs used in analysis.

External Methods

External methods rely on background knowledge from sources outside the given graph. Examples include other knowledge graphs, constraint specifications, or human expertise. The success of such methods depends heavily on the quality and compatibility of the external sources with the target graph or assertion.
