Crowdsourcing-based approaches introduce human expertise into the knowledge graph cleaning process. These techniques complement automated methods by enabling tasks such as validating and correcting assertion errors, analyzing results, or even designing new cleaning strategies.

A key distinction among approaches lies in the type of crowd involved and the complexity of the assigned tasks. Some methods rely entirely on human evaluation, while others integrate human input to support semi-automatic workflows.

Anonymous crowds (paid micro tasks)

These crowds consist of individuals without specialized knowledge. They are typically recruited through crowdsourcing platforms such as Amazon Mechanical Turk or Microworkers. Tasks assigned to anonymous crowds are usually short, simple, and independent — often referred to as paid microtasks. Such tasks include answering basic questions, validating assertions, or identifying obvious data issues.

Availability Service platforms: Crowdsourcing marketplaces
Task Difficulty Simple: Basic human abilities & knowledge
Task Duration Short: solvable in hours/days, atomic (i.e., no correlation to other tasks)

  • Analyze basic data/knowledge & clean errors

Domain Experts

Domain experts are individuals with specialized expertise in relevant knowledge domains or in knowledge graph technologies. They are sourced through expert platforms or engaged via scientific challenges. Tasks for experts are more complex, potentially continuous, and require the ability to interpret domain-specific semantics, analyze graph structures, or refine knowledge models.

The distinction summarized in Figure 3 — adapted from [30] — emphasizes how human input can be tailored to specific cleaning needs. Simpler, distributable tasks can be efficiently handled by anonymous crowds, while expert tasks benefit from focused knowledge and deeper reasoning capabilities.

While human-in-the-loop approaches provide valuable flexibility and semantic depth, they also face challenges in scalability and cost. Future research may focus on better integration of human insight into automated workflows and on tools that make this integration more efficient and user-friendly.
