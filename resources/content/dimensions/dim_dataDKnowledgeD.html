Another important dimension of cleaning approaches is whether they follow a data-driven or knowledge-driven workflow.

Data-driven Approaches

Data-driven methods rely on statistical analysis of background knowledge to detect or correct errors. They make predictions based on observed patterns and distributions in the data, making them highly dependent on the availability and quality of that data.

While often scalable—especially with large knowledge graphs—data-driven approaches can struggle in domains with significant variability or underrepresented areas, such as tail verticals: niche domains with sparse data coverage. Additionally, these methods may unintentionally reinforce existing biases present in the dataset.

Knowledge-driven Approaches

Knowledge-driven methods, also known as symbolic approaches, use explicit domain knowledge to guide error detection and correction. This knowledge may come from formal specifications, logic rules, or human expertise.

While such approaches can be highly effective in handling tail verticals and domains with many natural deviations, acquiring the required domain knowledge is often challenging. Moreover, these methods typically offer less scalability than their data-driven counterparts.
