Anatomy of Knowledge Cleaning Ontology (AKCO): Description back to ToC

The Anatomy of Knowledge Cleaning Ontology (AKCO) represents relevant aspects of knowledge cleaning assertion errors in knowledge graphs. The correctness of a knowledge graph is essential for its successful application. Knowledge cleaning, i.e., error detection and correction, aims to improve this correctness. A knowledge graph, typically, represents most knowledge as assertions, and commonly, three types of assertions are used: instance, property-value, and equality assertions. Each assertion type has unique features, requiring specific approaches to target their syntax or semantics and evaluate them. Gaining an overview of the possibilities is essential before implementing knowledge cleaning, since approaches have dependencies that need to be understood. For example, integrity constraints could be applied to detect if a knowledge graph conforms to certain specifications. However, to obtain integrity constraints, it is essential to have domain knowledge. This knowledge could be provided by an ontology that covers the assertions or by domain experts. If those sources are not available or are of insufficient quality, another cleaning technique might be better suited for a given knowledge graph. The AKCO allows the representation of all relevant aspects of a cleaning process, providing insights into dependencies and relevant related aspects. This organization enables knowledge graph maintainers to focus solely on the aspects that are best suited for cleaning a knowledge graph, considering its features, user requirements, and additional knowledge sources. The AKCO already contains over 100 instances (named individuals) that represent various facts of knowledge cleaning, including different types of background knowledge, cleaning techniques, dimensions, and a vast number of cleaning approaches. These instances provide a foundational understanding of the different aspects of knowledge cleaning and offer a selection of approaches.
