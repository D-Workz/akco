Contextual graphs

Contextual graphs are dynamically constructed subgraphs from a knowledge graph, built to support a specific task such as error detection or correction. These subgraphs are composed of assertions deemed relevant to the targeted task.

A contextual graph may be derived from the global context of a knowledge graph, capturing broad structures or equivalence relations (e.g., [Valdestilhas et al., 2017]; [de Melo, 2013]; [Raad et al., 2018]), or from the local context, focusing on a neighborhood or specific properties (e.g., [Symeonidou et al., 2011]; [Pernelle et al., 2013]; [Fan et al., 2015]; see Section: Context-based Techniques).

Contextual graphs can also be the result of reasoning over assertions using an ontology, which introduces entailed knowledge into the subgraph (see Section: Ontology-based Techniques).
