Formal and syntax specifications

Formal and syntactic specifications are typically a specific type of external background knowledge. They define desired properties of a knowledge graph, including expected structure, domain-specific syntax rules, and semantic constraints on properties. These specifications are defined independently of the graph’s actual data and can be used to validate or correct assertions.

Common formal specifications include: ontologies, integrity constraints, and logic rules. While often external, ontologies can also be embedded within the knowledge graph, in which case they serve as internal background knowledge.

Examples of syntactic specifications include the RDF specification and serialization standards such as Turtle syntax.
